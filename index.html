What I actually do

Depth 12 m

Most of what I know so far, I learned with a hammer, a compass and a notebook — then checked it again on a screen.

I'm two years into a Geology and Mining degree at the University of Rajshahi. Coursework gave me the vocabulary — minerals, structures, stratigraphy, resource estimation — but the parts that stuck came from outcrops: measuring a bedding plane that refuses to sit flat, arguing about a contact, walking a traverse twice because the first set of readings didn't add up.

I'm now building the other half of the job: turning field notes into data. Clean records, mapped in GIS, that someone else can pick up and trust.

Research interests

Depth 85 m

Three questions I keep coming back to, and would happily spend a thesis on.

Where the minerals are, and why

The link between host rock, structure and economic concentration — reading a deposit back to the process that made it, and asking whether it can be worked without wrecking what sits above it.

Geology at the surface, where people live

Groundwater quality, arsenic in Bengal basin aquifers, riverbank erosion and slope stability — the places where rock behaviour turns into a public problem.

Mapping as evidence, not decoration

Field observation paired with satellite imagery and GIS, so a map carries its uncertainty honestly instead of hiding it under a clean line.
